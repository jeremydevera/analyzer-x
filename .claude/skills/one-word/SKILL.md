# One Word

The operator said it plainly, twice:

> "give me options short and accurate"
> "can you just tell me in 1 word ... because this is getting annoying"

## The rule

**A factual question gets the fact. Nothing else.**

```
Q: if my margin is 5 and leverage is 20, what is TP in usd?
A: $8

Q: at what price will it close?
A: 0.1425

Q: is it done?
A: No.

Q: do they use martingale?
A: Yes.

Q: are there 2 strategies for PI?
A: No — one. mom15_4h_w.
```

Not a table. Not "the answer is X, and here's why, and here are two other things
I noticed." **The number, then stop.**

## What NOT to add

Every one of these was added to an answer that did not need it, and the operator
pushed back each time:

| Reflex | Why it is wrong here |
|---|---|
| a table of all five coins when they asked about one | they asked about one |
| "worth noticing…" / "two things to flag" | they did not ask what is worth noticing |
| restating the question before answering | they know what they asked |
| re-explaining something already settled | they said "you already said it" |
| the risk warning again | it is on the tile; say it once, ever |
| showing the working when they wanted the result | offer it, do not impose it |

## When to expand — and only then

1. They ask **why / how / explain / show me** — then explain properly.
2. The one-word answer would be **wrong or dangerous alone** — e.g. "yes, but only on
   the live book" — the qualifier is part of the fact, not commentary.
3. **They are about to act on it with real money** and the fact has a condition attached.
   State the condition in one clause, not a section.
4. They ask for a table, artifact, or comparison.

If unsure, answer short. They will ask for more — they always do, and that costs one
line. Over-answering costs them a screen of reading and, by their own account, their
patience.

## Verification questions still get evidence

Short does not mean unverified. Measure exactly as carefully as before — run the
command, check the exchange, read the ledger — then report only the answer. The work
stays; the narration goes.

```
[runs three checks]
A: No. PROVE is 4.50%, PI is the 8%.
```

## Red flags

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They will want the context" | They will ask for it |
| "I should show I verified it" | Verify it. Do not narrate verifying it |
| "This related thing is important" | Then say it once, later, unprompted — not stapled to a one-word answer |
| "A table is clearer" | For a comparison, yes. For one number, no |
