## The strategies

Almost every chapter builds the same kind of strategy: a model scores every stock each day with its predicted next-day return, then Zipline or backtrader buys the 25 highest and shorts the 25 lowest. Chapter by chapter:

- **`02_market_and_fundamental_data`, `03_alternative_data`**: data only (NASDAQ ITCH order book rebuild, Algoseek minute bars, SEC filings, scrapers). No strategy.
- **`04_alpha_factor_research`** (`04_single_factor_zipline`): short-term reversal. Factor = (last 21-day return - its average over the last 252 days) / its standard deviation. Among the 1,000 stocks with the highest 30-day dollar volume, buy the 25 lowest and short the 25 highest (`N_LONGS = N_SHORTS = 25`), equal weight, rebalanced each Monday at the open, Jan 01, 2015 to Jan 01, 2018. Costs `commission.PerShare(cost=.01)` and Zipline's volume-share slippage. Also Kalman filters, wavelets, TA-Lib, Alphalens factor checks.
- **`05_strategy_evaluation`**: the same reversal factor, 2013 to 2017, top 500 by dollar volume, 50 longs (`01_backtest_with_trades`, commission $0.00075 a share, minimum $0.01, volume-share slippage with `volume_limit=0.0025`); then weights chosen by maximum-Sharpe mean-variance (`02_backtest_with_pf_optimization`, 252 days of returns); pyfolio, Kelly rule.
- **`06_machine_learning_process`**: theory (mutual information, bias-variance, time-series cross-validation). No strategy.
- **`07_linear_models`**: predict each stock's 1-day forward return (`target_1d`; 5, 10 and 21 days also built) from lagged returns over 1, 5, 10, 21, 42, 63 days, RSI, Bollinger band distances, ATR, MACD, dollar-volume rank, 2013 to 2017, the 100 most traded. Ordinary least squares, Ridge (alpha from `np.logspace(-4, 4, 9)` and 5x those), Lasso; logistic regression for up/down (C from `np.logspace(-5, 5, 11)`). Rolling folds: train 63 days, test 10 days, gap 1 day, `n_splits = int(3 * YEAR / 10)`.
- **`08_ml4t_workflow`**: three backtests of chapter 7's Lasso predictions: a vectorised one (15 long / 15 short by predicted return, held one day, next day's open-to-open return), backtrader (`n_positions=25`, `min_positions=20`, $10,000), and Zipline with the model retrained inside the backtest (`SGDRegressor`, `TRAINING_PERIOD = 252 * 2`, retrained Monday, Wednesday, Friday, 25 longs and 25 shorts, at least 15, 2015 to 2017, 10 features such as 63- and 215-day returns, the reversal factor and a 252-day price trend). Also `01_multiple_testing/deflated_sharpe_ratio.py`.
- **`09_time_series_models`** (`06`, `07`): cointegrated pairs. Every 3 months from Dec 31, 2016 to Jun 30, 2019, test stock-vs-ETF pairs over the past 2 years (Engle-Granger p below 0.05 AND Johansen trace above its 95% value, 15.4943 for none); hedge ratio from a Kalman filter; z-score of the spread over a rolling 2x half-life window; enter at |z| above 2, exit when z crosses 0; trade the next 6 months, entries in the first 3; equal dollar per pair, backtrader, $1,000,000. Also ARIMA, GARCH, VAR (no trading).
- **`10_bayesian_machine_learning`**: Bayesian Sharpe comparison, rolling regression, stochastic volatility. No strategy.
- **`11_decision_trees_random_forests`**: Japanese stocks (Stooq, 250 most traded of 941). Random forest (LightGBM), horizons 1, 5, 10, 21 days, train lengths 5 years, 3 years, 1 year, 126 and 63 days, tests 5 or 21 days; the notebook's note: `min_samples_leaf` of 500 or 1,000 and 100-200 trees did best. The signal = the average prediction of the 5 best settings. Long 25 / short 25, at least 15, commission 0.05 a share (minimum 1), no slippage. Model chosen on 2010 to 2017; traded 2018 to 2019.
- **`12_gradient_boosting_machines`**: the book's main US strategy. 1,000 largest US stocks; features include returns over 1, 5, 10, 21, 42, 63 days, NATR, PPO, Bollinger bands, RSI, MACD, sector and calendar dummies; LightGBM and CatBoost tuned on 2015/16 as validation (train 4.5 years or 1 year, test 63 or 21 days, horizons 1, 5, 21 days); then predictions for 2017 never used in tuning. Long the 25 highest and short the 25 lowest predicted 1-day returns, at least 20 each side, daily, commission $0.001 a share, zero slippage. Plus an **intraday** model (`10`, `11`): NASDAQ-100 minute bars Feb 2016 to Dec 2017, predict the next 1-minute return with LightGBM (`num_leaves=16`, `min_data_in_leaf=500`, `feature_fraction=.8`, 250 rounds), 23 folds of 1 year train / 1 month test.
- **`13_unsupervised_learning`**: PCA risk factors, eigen-portfolios, clustering, and HRP: chapter 12's LightGBM longs (25, at least 20) weighted three ways, equal, hierarchical risk parity, and Markowitz maximum-Sharpe, commission $0.001 a share (minimum $1), zero slippage.
- **`14`-`16` (text)**: sentiment (TextBlob, Twitter, Yelp), LDA topics on earnings calls and news, word2vec on SEC filings. Text features only, no trading backtest in what I read.
- **`17_deep_learning`**: a feed-forward network on chapter 12's features, 995 US stocks, 2010 to 2017; layers tried `(16, 8), (32, 16), (32, 32), (64, 32)`, tanh, dropout 0, 0.1, 0.2; 12 folds of 4 years train / 3 months test; the 5 best settings by median daily IC in the first 24 months; long 25 / short 25 (at least 10) daily.
- **`18_convolutional_neural_nets`**: "CNN-TA": 15 indicators (RSI, WMA, EMA, SMA, CCI, CMO, MACD, PPO, ROC, ADX, NATR, WILLR, ADOSC, MOM...) each over periods 6 to 20 days, laid out as a 15 x 15 image, 500 most traded US stocks over rolling 5-year windows; a two-layer CNN (16 and 32 filters, dense 32, dropout 0.25 / 0.5) predicting the next day's return; 16 folds of 5 years train / 5 weeks test; long 25 / short 25.
- **`19_recurrent_neural_nets`**: stacked LSTM over the last 52 weekly returns plus ticker and month embeddings, predicting next week's return (train to 2016, test 2017); an LSTM on SEC filing text predicting returns.
- **`20_autoencoders_for_conditional_risk_factors`**: the Gu-Kelly-Xiu conditional autoencoder over stock characteristics (beta, momentum, dollar volume, idiosyncratic volatility, illiquidity, industry momentum ...), rank-normalised, `n_factors = 3` in the cell I read, predicting next-period returns, data to 2019.
- **`21_gans_for_synthetic_time_series`**: TimeGAN synthetic price data. No strategy (not read: the notebook did not download).
- **`22_deep_reinforcement_learning`**: a double DQN agent trading AAPL long / flat / short, state = returns over 1, 2, 5, 10, 21 days, STOCHRSI, MACD, ATR, stochastic, ultimate oscillator; network `(256, 256)`, `learning_rate = 0.0001`, `gamma = .99`, `batch_size = 4096`, `max_episodes = 1000`, each episode a random 252-day year; cost `trading_cost_bps = 1e-3` (0.1% per position change) plus `time_cost_bps = 1e-4` (0.01% a day).
- **`24_alpha_factor_library`**: TA-Lib indicator zoo, common factors and the "101 formulaic alphas", judged with Alphalens. No trading backtest.

## How it tests them

- **Splits**: the book's own rule is a rolling time-series split with a gap (`MultipleTimeSeriesCV`), and chapters 11, 12 and 17 do keep a final period away from tuning (Japan 2018-2019, US 2017, US last 12 months). That part is sound.
- **But test data leaks into choices in several places**:
  - chapter 19's LSTMs pass the 2017 test set as `validation_data` to `EarlyStopping` and `ModelCheckpoint(monitor='val_loss')`, so the test year picks the stopping epoch;
  - chapter 8's vectorised and backtrader runs use the Lasso alpha with the best rank correlation over the very predictions they then trade (`data_prep.py`: `best_alpha = predictions.groupby('alpha')...idxmax()`);
  - chapter 12 picks its universe as "1,000 stocks with highest market cap" over the whole sample (the notebook says so: "we simplify and use the entire sample period");
  - chapter 22's environment rescales the whole AAPL history at once (`scale(self.data)`) and samples training years from all of it, with no held-out year.
- **Costs**: mostly a commission per share and **zero slippage** (`FixedSlippage(spread=0.00)` in chapters 11, 12, 13, 17, 18). Commission is $0.001 a share in chapters 12 and 13, zero in chapter 8's Zipline run (`PerShare(cost=0)`), commented out in chapters 17 and 18 and in backtrader (`# cerebro.broker.addcommissioninfo`), absent in the pairs backtest. Chapters 4 and 5 use Zipline's volume-share slippage with $0.01 and $0.00075 a share. No short-borrow cost. The intraday notebook says outright: *"we will assume throughout that we can always buy (sell) at the first (last) trade price for a given bar at no cost and without market impact."* Only chapter 22 charges a flat 0.1% per trade.
- **Baselines**: pyfolio prints alpha and beta against the S&P 500; the DQN agent is compared with buy-and-hold of AAPL. There is no random-entry or random-ranking baseline.

## What matters for us

Our Gate measurement: the average trade earns about $0.00 before fees, fees plus the buy-sell gap cost about $0.23 per $100 traded, so fees are the whole loss, and chosen strategies did no better than random entries on days they were not chosen on. This book's results would not survive that. Its daily long-short books turn over 100-130% of capital a day and are charged about nothing (zero slippage, $0.001 a share or less), so even the best clean out-of-sample figure (chapter 12, 8.0% a year, Sharpe 0.61 over 11 months) would vanish under a 0.15-0.25% round trip: at 127% daily turnover that is roughly 0.2-0.3% of capital a day, about 50-80% a year. Its intraday model is the clearest case: the best tenth of its picks earned 0.37 basis points a minute before costs, a few hundred times less than one Gate round trip. The out-of-sample periods are short (11-23 months), there is no random baseline, and the one model tested on two clean unseen years (Japan) lost money. This lines up with our own finding.

Ideas worth testing on our crypto data:
1. **Rank, don't threshold: a market-neutral basket.** Each day (or 4 hours), rank every Gate contract by one score, hold the top N long and bottom N short in equal size. Our tests have been single-coin entries; a cross-coin rank cancels the market's own move. The cost check is easy to state in advance: holding time must be long enough that the expected spread between top and bottom beats 2 x our round trip.
2. **The short-term reversal factor** from chapter 4 (last 21 days' return against its 252-day average, in standard deviations), applied across coins weekly, with a random-ranking baseline: cheap to test, low turnover, a published effect in stocks.

## Doubts

- `08_ml4t_workflow/02_vectorized_backtest.ipynb` picks positions with `.rank(axis=1, ascending=False) > N_LONG`, which selects every stock EXCEPT the top 15 among positive predictions (and the same for shorts); it looks inverted. I did not run it, so this is a reading of the code only.
- The pyfolio tables are HTML outputs; I pulled their numbers by stripping the HTML, so a column could be misaligned. The ordering ("In-sample / Out-of-sample / All" vs "All / In-sample / Out-of-sample") differs between notebooks, and I quoted each in its own order.
- In the chapter 22 log, *"Agent: 48.2% (96.8%) | Market: 23.8% ( 13.1%) | Wins: 56.0%"* at episode 880: I could not tell from the cell which of the two numbers is the 100-episode average; all of it is in-sample.
