## What it is

This is the front page of a finance professor's blog. It holds two long posts about stocks, not about trading systems. The first (Nov 09, 2026) splits "the AI trade" into four groups (suppliers, builders, product firms, customers) and shows how they were hit differently in a market drop. The second (Nov 03, 2026) defends the author's value for Anthropic and then spends most of its length on one idea: the "cost of capital" (the return you could earn elsewhere) is often set far too high. It says nothing about trading costs, backtesting, overfitting or leverage; its value for us is a few ideas about uncertainty, hurdle rates, "promised versus delivered" returns and acting without timing the market. The last ~1,500 characters are a menu of old posts and archive links.

## Claims we could test on our own data

These are tests on this repo's crypto candles. The source never tested any of them; each is a way to check a claim it makes, in the repo's own terms. Our own test results would stand on their own, not on this source.

1. "Technical indicators (price and volume) may be better attuned to detecting mood and momentum shifts" (lesson 32). Test: run momentum and volume-spike rules on 15m-1d candles and compare profit after fees with random entries on the same coin and days, chosen on older days and judged on unseen days.
2. "Set a trigger price and automate the buy; a limit order beats timing it by hand" (lessons 36, 37). Test: on the waiting-order engine, compare buying at the signal candle's close with a resting buy a set percentage lower; count fills, missed trades and profit after fees.
3. "Do not wait for the bottom; you may never hit it" (lesson 35). Test: after a 10%+ drop on a coin, how often does price fall further below the first buy level before it recovers, against how often a lower resting order never fills.
4. "Sell a portion at each correction phase; you give up upside but are less exposed" (lessons 33, 34). Test: on trending coins, compare holding with selling a fixed share at each X% rise, measured by profit and worst dip.
5. "In a crisis the market marks down everything, then discerning buyers can pick the wrongly marked-down names" (lesson 43). Test: on days BTC falls more than a set size, group coins by how far they fell and compare their next 1-7 day returns.
6. "One theme hides opposite moves in different groups" (lessons 23, 26). Test: split coins by type (the repo's contract kind or a size ranking) and measure how far group returns differ week to week, including in the week of a big drop.
7. "A bar that is too high finds nothing, or finds scams/flukes" (lessons 73, 90, 91). Test: raise the minimum win rate or profit filter step by step on stored strategies and measure how many rows survive on the older days and how many of those still win on unseen days.
8. "Promised returns are fiction; delivered returns are far lower" (lessons 84, 85, 88). Test: compare each stored strategy's chosen-days profit with its unseen-days profit and report the average drop.
9. "Few firms beat their cost of capital" (lesson 89). Test, as an analogy: the share of strategies with positive profit after fees on unseen days, with 20+ trades.
10. "A bigger margin of safety trades type 1 errors for type 2 errors" (lesson 58). Test: sweep a minimum-edge threshold and plot bad trades avoided against good trades skipped.
11. "Many positions let you hold riskier items; do not put it all in one name" (lessons 60, 61). Test: equal-weight 1, 5, 20 and 50 coins running the same rule and measure how the worst dip and losing streak change with the count.
12. "Errors on key inputs point the same way" (lesson 51). Test: measure how often strategies on different coins lose on the same day, to see if losses are bunched.
