## What it is

This is the front page of the code for a book about building machine-learning trading strategies, from getting data to running a strategy live. It lists 27 chapters and nine case studies (ETFs, crypto perpetuals, intraday stocks, options, FX, futures, stock factors), and says each case study is carried through the same pipeline: data, labels, features, models, backtest, costs, risk, and a final deployment check. Most of the page is a menu (chapter table, install steps, Docker images, course adverts), so it does not teach by itself; the useful teaching is in one-paragraph descriptions of what each chapter covers and in the two chapter guides (16 and 18). The central idea is that the process is what gives you an edge, and that a backtest is a test that tries to break the strategy, not a proof. The README gives almost no numbers about results; the results live in notebooks that were not part of the text I was given.

## What it says on costs, overfitting, testing on unseen data, position size, leverage and stops

Costs: the most covered topic. It says costs are not a backtest footnote but a limit on the whole workflow, that they must be split into explicit, implicit and capacity parts, that they change with the regime and the time of day, that a ladder of models runs from spread-only to linear slippage to square-root impact, that cautious defaults are right when you have no fills, and that fee rates should be tested against real fills afterward. It also says a flat fee rate (used in Chapter 16) is later replaced by a size- and volume-based model. It gives no fee or spread numbers.

Overfitting: it says that picking the best of many variants inflates the Sharpe ratio even with no edge, that the test must cover the whole set that was searched, and it names the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, the probability of backtest overfitting, the Rademacher Anti-Serum, White's Reality Check, BH-FDR, and conformal prediction. It gives no thresholds (for example, no minimum number of trades).

Testing on unseen data: it describes an evidence boundary between tuning and evaluation, walk-forward cross-validation "throughout", and holdout failure modes in Chapter 20. The README does not state a split size or how long a test window should be.

Position size: only named, not explained. Chapter 17 lists the Kelly criterion, conformal position sizing, Hierarchical Risk Parity and mean-variance optimization; Chapter 16 lists Kelly sizing "that reads its own realized P&L". No rule or number is given.

Leverage: not covered (the word does not appear in the three texts).

Stops: not covered as stop-loss rules (the word "stop" appears only in unrelated senses). The nearest items are triple-barrier labels in Chapter 7, a drawdown circuit breaker in Chapter 16, and kill switches in Chapter 19, which are named but not explained here.

## Claims we could test on our own data

1. "dollar bars deliver the best return normality" (Chapter 3). Build dollar bars (a bar closes after a fixed amount of money traded) from our 1-minute crypto candles and compare the shape of returns (skew, fat tails, a normality test) with time bars on the same coins.
2. "What looks like a stellar strategy on a gross basis often becomes unprofitable or marginal after realistic costs." Take our stored strategies, compute profit before and after each trade's own cost, and count how many positive-before rows turn negative-after. This repo has already seen this; a fresh count would confirm it.
3. "breakeven costs that vary widely by asset class". For each coin and kind (crypto, stock tokens, metals), find the extra cost per trade at which total profit reaches zero, using our per-minute cost store, and compare the spread between kinds.
4. Spread estimated from candle data matches the true spread (notebook 02). We hold the app's own recorded order books for recent days, so we could compute the two candle-based estimators and compare them with the recorded gap coin by coin.
5. "selecting across many variants inflates the apparent Sharpe even when no edge exists". Choose the best of N random entry rules on older days and measure the same pick on unseen days; the gap should grow with N. Our random-entry runs already give a base to start from.
6. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio changes the verdict. Apply it to our picks using the real number of trials we tried (for example, the number of combinations measured), and see which of the "passed" rows stop passing.
7. "The gap between them accumulates rather than oscillating" (vectorized vs event-driven). Compare our fast grid walk with the full engine on the same rows over time and see whether the profit difference drifts one way.
8. "a threshold sweep whose surface is flat in one direction and not the other" (RSI on BTC/USDT daily). Sweep RSI entry and exit levels on BTC 1d on Gate data and see whether profit changes smoothly along one axis and sharply along the other.
9. "the critical tradeoff between signal quality and transaction costs at different rebalancing frequencies". Compare the same idea on 15m, 30m, 1h, 4h and 1d, net of per-minute costs, and see where costs overtake the edge.
10. "overlay effectiveness turns out to be strategy-specific". Add a simple drawdown stop on several of our strategies and check whether it helps some and hurts others.
11. "gradient boosting the strongest tabular model in most case studies". Only testable if we build a model; it would need a labelled crypto set and a linear baseline (lesson 22 in the lessons list) and is a larger job.
