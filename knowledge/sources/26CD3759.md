## What it is

This is the personal home page of Aswath Damodaran, who teaches corporate finance and valuation at NYU Stern. It is mostly a hub: a short introduction, a monthly table of implied equity risk premium figures, links to data files and spreadsheets, and lists of his classes, papers and books. It is about valuing companies and about the extra return investors ask for holding stocks. It has no trading strategies, no backtests and nothing on crypto. The saved online-class page is a menu of free class playlists with one short description each. For this repo it is background reading on valuation, not a source of rules for the crypto backtests.

## Numbers and rules it gives

> "the website is broken down into four sections"

> "Implied ERP on November 1, 2026 = 4.41 % (Trailing 12 month, with adjusted payout); 3.79% (Trailing 12 month cash yield); 5.81% (Average CF yield last 10 years); 3.43% (Net cash yield); 3.39% (Normalized Earnings & Payout)"

## Claims we could test on our own data

Almost none. Every claim on the page is about company valuation, stock market risk premiums, ESG, class design or his own writing, and none is a claim a backtest on this repo's crypto candles could confirm or refute. The big market delusion paper says big markets plus overconfident people can create over pricing, but the page gives no rule or number that a backtest could check. Two loose ideas of ours (not claims from the page): the US treasury rate it prints (4.94% on November 1, 2026) could be a hurdle that a backtest's yearly return is compared with; and the spread between its five premium methods (3.39% to 5.81%) shows how much a result can move with the method, which is the same worry as one result depending on one setting. Neither is tested here.

## What I did not read, and doubts

I read all 1,316 words of the home page text and all of the saved online-class page (onlineclass.htm). I did not read any linked page, spreadsheet, paper, video, book or the NYU certificate page, so I say nothing about what they contain. The earlier summary file says the same: the page gives no price for any class, so the "free" label rests only on the home page heading.
> "Online classes (Free and NYU Certificate)"

This page is mostly a menu and a data table; it holds no trading method, so the note is short on purpose. It is about stocks and company valuation, not crypto.

Doubts: (1) The home page says the data update was January 9, 2026 and the next is January 2027, yet it prints November 1, 2026 premiums, so the premium block is refreshed monthly while the data files are yearly. (2) The link labelled Day-to-day ERP in March 2026 sits under a November 2026 heading, which looks like a stale label. (3) The intro opinion that three industries are in need of change is the author's view, not a tested claim.
> "that are all big, inefficiently run and deserve to be disrupted"

(4) The ESG remark is the author's own judgment and has no number behind it on this page. (5) The text has small typing errors (for example "alrready", "ccessible", "valuaton"), so quote it only from the saved file. (6) The page promotes his own books, app and classes; treat that as promotion, not evidence.
