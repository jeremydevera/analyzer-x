## What it is

This is the front page of Rob Carver's open-source engine for testing and running rule-based futures trading in Python. The README says it is the open-source version of his own testing and trading engine, built on the framework in his book "Systematic Trading". Most of the page is housekeeping: who maintains the project, how to install it, where the other documents are, and a long legal warning. It teaches almost nothing about HOW to run or judge a backtest. The real teaching is in the guides it links to (backtesting guide, data guide), which this note did not read. The page says plainly that it is for people who already write Python.

## Claims we could test on our own data

The README makes very few claims a crypto backtest can check; the book and guides it points to would hold the real claims. Three that follow from its text:

1. "Backtested results are no guarantee of future performance." We can test this directly. Take the strategies chosen on older days in Backtest v2 or v4 and compare their profit on the older days with their profit on days they never saw. This repo has done this several times and found the unseen days lose money after fees, so the claim holds on our data. A fresh check would use the newest unseen days.
2. Leverage may lose "all your money, and still owing more". On a crypto exchange the loss is capped at margin, so "owing more" is not expected here. We can test the first half: count trades in the stored backtests where a gap or fast move would pass the liquidation price before the stop fires (at 20x, a stop close to the liquidation wall).
3. The project "implements all the optimisation and system design principles in his books". We cannot test this from the README. It would need the book and the guides, and then a comparison of his position-size and cost rules with the ones this repo uses.
