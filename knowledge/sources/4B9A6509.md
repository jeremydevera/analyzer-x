## Claims we could test on our own data

1. The 200-day-average example: buy when price crosses above it, sell when it crosses below. Test on the repo's daily crypto candles (all coins, 1d), long only and also long and short, with the repo's real costs (0.075% fee each way, the minute's own gap, funding), then judge it on days not used to choose anything and compare it with random entries on the same coins. The repo's trend family uses 20, 50 and 100 day windows (per the repo's own notes), so 200 would be a new setting; the page claims nothing about results, so this only checks that the "super simple" rule is not free money.
2. "Algo strategies can be validated before risking capital by testing rules on years of historical data." Test it as: choose rules on an older stretch, then measure them on a newer stretch they never saw, after all costs, against random picks. If rules that pass on history lose on the newer stretch, the claim that history testing "validates" is refuted for this market. The repo's notes already record such runs where picks chosen on older days did not hold on unseen days, so this would be a re-check, not new ground.

Everything else on the page (consistency, removing emotion, discretionary traders cannot test themselves, free vs paid) is about the trader or the sales offer, not about prices, so a backtest cannot confirm or refute it.
