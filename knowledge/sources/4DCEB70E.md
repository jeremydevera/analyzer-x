## What it is

This is the front page of Quantocracy, a site that collects links to quant-trading blog posts from about 107 blogs and shows the newest 50, each with a two-to-four sentence excerpt that stops mid-sentence with "(...)". It teaches no method of its own; every lesson below is a teaser from someone else's post, and most excerpts stop before they give the result. The strongest thread on this page is a run of posts that test an advertised rule and report the gap between the claimed and the measured win rate (for example "72.17% Claimed, 65.9% Measured"). The rest is a sidebar of blog names, social links and four sponsors.

## What it says on costs, overfitting, testing on unseen data, position size, leverage and stops

Costs: only in passing, inside excerpts. The Triple RSI test counted costs ("after costs"), the Bitcoin abnormal-day rule lost money even "before costs", the FX machine-learning signal was real but could not pay the spread, one series moves from tuning weights "on a pre cost basis" to adding costs, and the capacity excerpt says return per dollar is not the same as how much money a strategy can take. No cost figures are given.

Overfitting: touched by several excerpts but never explained. The opening-range seller admits "these settings are optimized"; one author wants an effect to "show up across factor variants"; one uses "pre-set checks" and "a control group"; one says most failed signals "existed only inside a measurement bias", and a real one survived "nineteen placebos". No method or threshold beyond "about 100" trades.

Testing on unseen data: the clearest lesson on the page. The RSI(2) claim has "a hard end date: 31 December 2006" and is tested on the 20 years after (80.7% against 84% claimed); Triple RSI worked on SPY 1994-2026 but before 1993 got "70.6% and No Gain"; and the month-end dollar trade was "real for a decade, unreliable since 2021". The page does not say whether any of these later years were kept fully out of the tuning.

Position size: only two mentions. One post sizes Bitcoin "from first principles rather than from a backtest alone" because its history holds one adoption run, and one shows a model asking for 0.03 of a contract when the broker wants whole contracts. No sizing rule is given.

Leverage: one excerpt asks the reader to "pick the correct level of leverage on a trading strategy" and links it to the spread of bootstrapped results; the Peak of Everything excerpt uses "leverage" about U.S. companies, not trading. No figure. (The tut1 fetch summary mentions a 2x version of a portfolio, but that is a tool summary, not the page.)

Stops: almost not covered. The RSI(2) rule has "no stop", the gold rule's target is "1.5 times the risk" with break-even at 40%, and the trend-filter excerpt says a monthly check "cannot react to a crash that happens in three weeks". No stop rule is taught.

## Claims we could test on our own data

1. Break-even at a 1.5x target is 40% before fees: our rows already carry TP, SL and the cost each trade paid, so we can work out break-even after Gate's 0.075% fee each way and the recorded gap, and show how far above 40% it moves (the repo's own rule says a 1.2%/1.2% pair needs 62.3%).
2. Bitcoin's abnormal days do not reward following them (all 9 settings lost, -29.98 bps a trade before costs): the excerpt does not define an abnormal day, so we would pick a definition first (for example the top and bottom 5% of daily moves on BTC_USDT 1d), buy or sell the next candle, and report the result before and after costs on BTC and the other big coins.
3. A buy-the-dip RSI(2) rule wins most trades: run an RSI(2)-under-a-level, no-stop, exit-on-bounce rule on every coin at 1h, 4h and 1d, and compare its win rate with its money after fees (the excerpt's point is that a high win rate can earn little).
4. The box rule (sell at yesterday's high, buy at yesterday's low, stay out of the middle) measured 32.8%: build it on Gate 1d highs and lows with 15m or 1h entries and minute exits.
5. Opening range breakout: crypto has no market open, but the stock tokens do (New York 9:30am), and UTC 00:00 could stand in for crypto; test a 5-minute range breakout on the 1m candles.
6. MA 9/21 crossing with a 200 MA filter and a 1.5x target: testable on every coin and timeframe in the grid, and the measured win rate compared with the 40% break-even and with the break-even after fees.
7. A monthly trend filter cannot dodge a three-week crash: compare a filter checked monthly with one checked daily on BTC around its fastest drops.
8. Correlations rise in stress: measure rolling correlation of each coin with BTC on days BTC fell hard against quiet days.
9. A signal can be real before costs and lose after costs: the repo's walk-forward work already found about $0.00 a trade before fees and -$0.23 after; the same before/after split can be printed for any new rule.
10. An edge can fade (month-end dollar "unreliable since 2021"): split any month-end or time-of-day crypto effect by year and see if it shrinks.
11. Pre-FOMC drift: `research/cq/us_news.json` already lists the Fed dates; buy BTC (and the stock tokens) at 4:00pm New York the day before and sell at 2:00pm on the day, on 1m candles, with every cost.
12. "is the move really over in 30 minutes" after a news release: use the same news file and the 1m candles to see whether price keeps going after the first 30 minutes of a CPI or jobs release.
13. Overnight versus daytime returns: crypto never closes, but Gate's stock tokens can be split into New York hours and the rest.
14. Touch about twice as often as finish: for levels X% away, count how often BTC's path touched the level within N days against how often it closed beyond it at day N.
15. Wanting "about 100" trades before saying anything: check how results of our own rows with 30, 50, 100 and 300 trades hold up on their unseen days.
