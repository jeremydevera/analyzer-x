## What it is

A five-hour, intermediate-level online course that teaches how to build trading strategies that act on signals, using
the R programming language and its quantstrat package. It is taught by Ilya Kipnis, described as a "Professional
Quantitative Analyst and R programmer", with Lore Dirick as collaborator. It has six chapters: trading basics, the
set-up quantstrat needs, indicators, signals, rules for entering and leaving trades, and reading the results (including
the Sharpe ratio). The practice data is SPY (a US stock-market fund) from 2000 through 2016. The saved page only
describes the course; it lists the exercise titles of chapter 1 and nothing deeper.

## Everything it teaches

0. The course as a whole: set up a quantstrat strategy, turn price data into indicators, make signals from how those
   indicators meet, simulate orders, then judge the results with numbers and charts.
   > "It will teach you how to set up a quantstrat strategy, apply transformations of market data called indicators, create signals based on the interactions of those indicators, and even simulate orders."
   > "Lastly, it will explain how to analyze your results both from statistical and visual perspectives."

1. **Chapter 1 - Trading basics.** What trading is, the two main styles (riding a move, "momentum", or betting a
   swing comes back, "oscillation"), the traps, overfitting, getting and charting price data, and one well-known
   indicator.
   > "In this chapter, you will learn the definition of trading, the philosophies of trading, and the pitfalls that exist in trading."
   > "This chapter covers both momentum and oscillation trading, along with some phrases to identify these types of philosophies."
   > "You will learn about overfitting and how to avoid it, obtaining and plotting financial data, and using a well-known indicator in trading."

2. **Chapter 1, exercise 1 - Why do people trade?** The reasons people trade at all (the page gives only the title).
   > "Why do people trade? 50 XP"

3. **Chapter 1, exercise 2 - Identifying types of trading philosophies, part I.** Telling the trading styles apart
   (title only; the chapter text says these are momentum and oscillation).
   > "Identifying types of trading philosophies - I"

4. **Chapter 1, exercise 3 - Identifying types of trading philosophies, part II.** More of the same (title only).
   > "Identifying types of trading philosophies - II 50 XP"

5. **Chapter 1, exercise 4 - Identifying types of trading philosophies, part III.** More of the same (title only).
   > "Identifying types of trading philosophies - III"

6. **Chapter 1, exercise 5 - Pitfalls of various trading systems.** The traps different kinds of trading systems fall
   into (title only; the traps themselves are not listed).
   > "Pitfalls of various trading systems 50 XP"

7. **Chapter 1, exercise 6 - How to prevent overfitting.** How to avoid a strategy that only fits the past it was
   built on (title only; the method is not given).
   > "How to prevent overfitting"

8. **Chapter 1, exercise 7 - Getting financial data.** Loading price data into R (title only).
   > "Getting financial data 50 XP"

9. **Chapter 1, exercise 8 - Plotting financial data.** Drawing a price chart (title only).
   > "Plotting financial data 100 XP"

10. **Chapter 1, exercise 9 - Adding indicators to financial data.** Putting an indicator on top of the prices (title
    only).
    > "Adding indicators to financial data 50 XP"

11. **Chapter 1, exercise 10 - Adding a moving average to financial data.** The "well-known indicator" of this chapter
    appears to be a moving average, the average price over the last N candles (title only).
    > "Adding a moving average to financial data 100 XP"

12. **Chapter 2 - A boilerplate for quantstrat strategies.** Before any strategy, quantstrat needs set-up: a time zone,
    a currency, the things being traded, and its record-keeping parts; the result is a reusable set-up file.
    > "Before building a strategy, the quantstrat package requires you to initialize some settings."
    > "You will cover a series of functions that deal with initializing a time zone, currency, the instruments you'll be working with, along with quantstrat's various frameworks that will allow it to perform analytics."
    > "Once this is done, you will have the knowledge to set up a quantstrat initialization file, and know how to change it."

13. **Chapter 3 - Indicators.** An indicator reshapes price data so its behaviour is easier to see, at the price of
    being late; both trend and swing (oscillation) indicators are used, ready-made ones from other libraries and one
    you write yourself.
    > "They are transformations of market data that allow a clearer understanding of its overall behavior, usually in exchange for lagging the market behavior."
    > "Here, you will be working with both trend types of indicators as well as oscillation indicators."
    > "You will also learn how to use pre-programmed indicators available in other libraries as well as implement one of your own."

14. **Chapter 4 - Signals.** A signal is when price meets an indicator, or two indicators meet each other; quantstrat
    has four kinds: a comparison, a crossover, a threshold and a formula.
    > "Signals are interactions of market data with indicators, or indicators with other indicators."
    > "There are four types of signals in quantstrat: sigComparison, sigCrossover, sigThreshold, and sigFormula."

15. **Chapter 5 - Rules.** What actually happens once a signal fires: how to enter and leave a trade and how to pass
    numbers to the part that decides the trade's size; it says it covers only "the gist" and points elsewhere for more.
    > "In this chapter, you'll learn how to shape your trading transaction once you decide to execute on a signal."
    > "This chapter will cover a basic primer on rules, and how to enter and exit positions."
    > "You'll also learn how to send inputs to order-sizing functions."
    > "By the end of this chapter, you'll learn the gist of how rules function, and where you can continue learning about them."

16. **Chapter 6 - Analyzing results.** Reading the strategy's trade statistics, seeing its performance over time, and
    working out the Sharpe ratio (profit measured against how much it swings) two ways.
    > "You will learn how to read vital trade statistics, and view the performance of your trading strategy over time."
    > "You will also learn how to get a reward to risk ratio called the Sharpe ratio in two different ways."
    > "This is the last chapter."

## Claims we could test on our own data

None with a number. The page sells a course; it makes no claim that any strategy makes money. Two of its ideas could
be checked on this app's Gate perpetual-futures candles, as ideas rather than claims:

- "Indicators lag the market" - the page says indicators help you see behaviour "usually in exchange for lagging the
  market behavior". How many candles a moving average turns after price turns could be measured per coin and
  timeframe, but that is a property, not a profit.
- "Momentum" against "oscillation" as the two styles - the app's stores already hold both kinds of strategy (trend
  followers and swing-back strategies), so the course adds no new test here.
