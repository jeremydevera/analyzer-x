# Quantopian forum archive (about 15,000 threads)

- **Link:** https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/
- **Type:** html (one index page: a table of thread titles, reply counts and dates; no post text)
- **Free or paid:** free to read as saved (a public archive page; no login, sign-up or price appears in the parts I read)
- **Words read:** all 141,835 words: about 12,100 on Nov 18, 2026 (see the last section), then the rest, characters 40,000 to 983,170, on Nov 19, 2026 by six agents in six slices (appendix at the end). Every listed title was checked word for word against the saved text.

## What it is

This is the front page of a copy of the Quantopian community forum, kept after Quantopian closed. It is one long table: each row is a thread title, how many replies it got and the date it was started, newest first (the first row is 30/10/2020, the last rows I saw are 21/08/2012). It holds no post text at all, so it teaches nothing directly: it only shows what members asked about, argued over or announced. Every "lesson" below is what a TITLE says or asks, never an answer that was checked; many titles are questions from beginners. It is not the Quantopian lecture series, though some titles announce lectures.

## Claims we could test on our own data

These come from titles, so each is a question the forum raised, not a finding it proved.

1. Claim (paraphrased from a title): More complex models win in the backtest and fail on unseen data. Test: in the Backtest v4 store (each strategy's older 70% and unseen 30%), group strategies by how many settings they have and compare older profit with unseen profit per group; the claim holds if the gap grows with the number of settings.
2. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Backtest results do not carry over to later results (the Q paper's subject). Test: over all v4 strategies, measure the rank link between older-days profit and unseen-days profit; near zero confirms it. The repo's earlier walk-forward checks point the same way, but this would put a number on it for every timeframe.
3. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Adding costs changes the verdict. Test: for Backtest v2 rows, count how many make money before fees and how many after the real per-minute book costs; compare with the flat default cost to see whether a flat cost is "realistic" (title 36).
4. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Better fills with more capital is a red flag. Test: price the same trades at $5 and at $50 margin through the recorded order book walk; fills should get worse, never better, as size grows.
5. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Stops can fill on prices that never traded. Test: compare stop exits settled on the candle with the same exits settled minute by minute (v2's minute rule) and count trades that change outcome.
6. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Daily-bar and minute-bar backtests differ. Test: run the same signal with bar-rule exits and minute-exact exits and report the dollar gap per timeframe.
7. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Max drawdown is wrong on no-trade days. Test: compute drawdown with and without the flat days in the equity line for a sample of rows and check the stored figure matches the full-day version.
8. Claim (paraphrased from a title): The look-back that won before will not win next. Test: for each coin and timeframe, pick the best look-back on the older days and check whether it is still the best, or above the median, on the newer days.
9. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Results change with how many past bars feed an indicator. Test: run the signals with the runner's 299-candle window and with the whole history and count signals that differ.
10. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Random picks are a fair comparison. Test: already in the repo's luck bars; repeat for any strategy family this source inspires.
11. Claim (paraphrased from a title): A long backtest can reject a system that works over short stretches. Test: nested windows (1, 3, 6 months, full history), as the still-working check does, and count rows that pass short windows but fail the full one.
12. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Robust strategies do not depend on exact settings. Test: for a chosen row, check whether its neighbours (nearby TP, SL and look-back) also make money.

### What these titles tell us

These are only titles: each one is a question or an opinion someone posted, not a proven answer, and a high reply count shows how much attention a thread got, not that anyone in it was right.

1. The most-replied thread in this whole slice is a strategy whose own title admits the catch: "VIX trading algorithm return 150% a year over past 5 years but has 50% drawdown from 2015 meltdown" drew 398 replies, so the crowd was drawn to big returns while the drop was already sitting in the headline.
2. Overfitting was named but rarely argued about: Quantopian's own study "All that Glitters Is Not Gold: Comparing Backtest and Out-of-Sample Performance on a Large Cohort of Trading Algorithms" got 76 replies, while "Great paper on overfitting your backtests" got 3 and the "Overfitting" lecture got 7 - and a post inviting people to tune settings with heat maps got 44, more than both of those together.
3. Costs were a running doubt: people asked "is slippage a silly idea?" (11 replies), "Why ever use 0 commission in backtesting" (8) and asked for a warning on backtest charts drawn without commissions (6), while the zero-commission Robinhood thread drew 135 replies.
4. Fills came up even more often than costs (33 titles against 27) - limit orders that the simulator filled or did not fill unlike a real market (19 replies on simulating non-marketable limit orders), partial fills, orders cancelled at the end of the day, and even "Backtest getting better fills at higher initial capital".
5. Several people checked their backtest against real trading and asked why they differ ("Backtest vs Live Trading Issues", "Live trading not tracking backtesting", "Backtesting to 6 months of live trading - a tearsheet analysis" with 9 replies, "A year of live trading" with 31).
6. Too-good numbers made people suspicious: "How often do you see 425% returns against the past 18 months?" (11), "Realistic Return Rates and Back-Test Numbers" (9), and a ~400% a year idea whose own title calls it "super-volatile and brittle".
7. A cluster of titles are blow-ups where losses went past the money in the account ("Algo going -22000%", "Why did the returns could go below -100%?", "Catastrophic Failure(~150% drawdown) on mean-reversion algo"); a loss bigger than the account can only come from borrowed money or short positions, so these read like tests with no limit on either, though only the threads themselves would say.
8. Stop losses appear in 22 titles, but nearly all ask how to code a trailing or fixed stop; the one title asking whether stops actually help ("How do stop-loss orders affect trading strategy performance?") got no replies.
9. People also argued over how the score itself is worked out - "backtester sharpe calculations" (12), a "Wrong Sharpe Ratio calculation" report, different Sharpe values in two tools - and a strategy pitched on its Sharpe alone ("Is this worth deploying capital? (2.5 sharpe)") drew 26 replies.
10. Whole topics are almost absent in these ten months of 2016: no title mentions cross-validation, a holdout set or Monte Carlo, Kelly sizing appears twice, and the only crypto title ("bitcoin trading") got no replies - the titles in this slice are almost all about US stocks and ETFs.

### What these titles tell us

Everything here is a thread TITLE: a question or an opinion someone posted, never a proven answer, and a reply count only shows how much attention a thread got, not whether anyone in it was right.

### What these titles tell us

These are only thread titles: they show what people asked and claimed in 2012-2015, not what turned out to be true, and none of the answers were read.

1. The question asked most often is "why do my test results disagree?": about ten titles ask why a minute test and a daily test of the same strategy give different results ("Why good daily backtests fail at minute backtests?", "Backtest results different in minute and daily mode", "testing on dailies doesn't make sense", 20 replies), and others compare a test with live trading or with other data ("how will algo backtest results differ from trading results", "Quantopian Backtesting In Comparison to Reality", "Why is backtest data off from other data sources?").
2. People kept posting test returns they did not believe themselves - "822910.1% Return...something can't be right??", "The "Hello World" algorithm makes a 4000% return?", "Serious questions: why are so many of the algos here returning +1000% over a few years?" (11 replies), "200,000% return - yeah, right!" - which says the community knew a huge test number usually meant a mistake, not an edge.
3. Overfitting is named rarely and directly only once ("Q-Open submitted strategy: curve-fit and leveraged", 7 replies), plus a few posts on data mining and bias with almost no replies ("Link investigating data mining bias in trading systems", no responses; "NYT on the dangers of data mining", 2), while titles about tuning settings drew much more talk ("Examples of Parameter Optimization?" 28, "Grid-searching for profitable cointegrating portfolios?" 28) - the tool to search was wanted more than the warning about searching.
4. Checking a strategy on data it was not built on barely appears: one walk-forward title (2 replies), one cross-validation title (1 reply), and no title says "out-of-sample" or "holdout" at all.
5. Costs were a steady worry: 25 titles on commissions, fees and taxes (the busiest "Default commission model very different from IB fees", 17) and 12 on slippage and the buy-sell gap ("Trade at the open slippage model", 21; "Bid/Ask spread data", 17), with one title even reporting "Better performance with commissions added" as something odd.
6. How an order really fills was asked again and again (21 titles, e.g. "Limit Order execution at varying prices" 23, "Limit Order, Market order, How real is this?" 14) - people did not trust that the tester filled orders the way a broker would.
7. Stops were the single most practical topic (30 titles): how to code a stop or trailing stop ("Usage & Syntax Of STOP Orders" 46, "Trailing Stop-Loss" 30) and complaints that stops did not fire ("Why don't stop losses execute?", "Stop Loss doesn't seem to work").
8. Leverage came up mostly as an accident to prevent - algorithms that overspent or went into debt ("Template: Limit Leverage in Your Algos" 23, "My overdraft", "How do I keep this from making my portfolio seriously overdrawn?") and leveraged ETFs being banned from the contest ("How to exclude leveraged ETFs from universe" 32).
9. Risk was mostly talked about as portfolio math (minimum variance portfolios, hedging, beta) and as doubts about the site's own risk numbers ("bug: risk metrics are miscalculated (overall metrics)", "Quantopian's Sharpe ratio does not match MorningStar/Yahoo" 15, "Inaccurate max drawdowns on no trade days/periods" 15); sizing by Kelly got three threads (13, 9 and 8 replies) and Monte Carlo three small ones.
10. Bitcoin and crypto were a side topic (10 titles, the busiest "Anyway to import Bitcoin data?" with 15 replies), because the platform traded US stocks; one title already asks how to trade bitcoin "after fees" without losing the starting money.
