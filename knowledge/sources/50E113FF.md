# Quantopian forum archive (about 15,000 threads)

- **Link:** https://quantopian-archive.netlify.app/forum/
- **Type:** html (one index page: a table of thread titles, reply counts and dates; no post text)
- **Free or paid:** free to read as saved (a public archive page; no login, sign-up or price appears in the parts I read)
- **Words read:** about 12,100 of 141,835 words (84,039 of 983,170 characters, 8.5%). Exact parts are listed in the last section.

## What it is

This is the front page of a copy of the Quantopian community forum, kept after Quantopian closed. It is one long table: each row is a thread title, how many replies it got and the date it was started, newest first (the first row is 30/10/2020, the last rows I saw are 21/08/2012). It holds no post text at all, so it teaches nothing directly: it only shows what members asked about, argued over or announced. Every "lesson" below is what a TITLE says or asks, never an answer that was checked; many titles are questions from beginners. It is not the Quantopian lecture series, though some titles announce lectures.

Every item is a thread title, so each one is a topic or a member's claim, not proven advice. "Where" gives the date the thread started and its reply count, as the index prints them.

## Claims we could test on our own data

These come from titles, so each is a question the forum raised, not a finding it proved.

1. Claim (paraphrased from a title): More complex models win in the backtest and fail on unseen data. Test: in the Backtest v4 store (each strategy's older 70% and unseen 30%), group strategies by how many settings they have and compare older profit with unseen profit per group; the claim holds if the gap grows with the number of settings.
2. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Backtest results do not carry over to later results (the Q paper's subject). Test: over all v4 strategies, measure the rank link between older-days profit and unseen-days profit; near zero confirms it. The repo's earlier walk-forward checks point the same way, but this would put a number on it for every timeframe.
3. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Adding costs changes the verdict. Test: for Backtest v2 rows, count how many make money before fees and how many after the real per-minute book costs; compare with the flat default cost to see whether a flat cost is "realistic" (title 36).
4. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Better fills with more capital is a red flag. Test: price the same trades at $5 and at $50 margin through the recorded order book walk; fills should get worse, never better, as size grows.
5. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Stops can fill on prices that never traded. Test: compare stop exits settled on the candle with the same exits settled minute by minute (v2's minute rule) and count trades that change outcome.
6. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Daily-bar and minute-bar backtests differ. Test: run the same signal with bar-rule exits and minute-exact exits and report the dollar gap per timeframe.
7. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Max drawdown is wrong on no-trade days. Test: compute drawdown with and without the flat days in the equity line for a sample of rows and check the stored figure matches the full-day version.
8. Claim (paraphrased from a title): The look-back that won before will not win next. Test: for each coin and timeframe, pick the best look-back on the older days and check whether it is still the best, or above the median, on the newer days.
9. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Results change with how many past bars feed an indicator. Test: run the signals with the runner's 299-candle window and with the whole history and count signals that differ.
10. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Random picks are a fair comparison. Test: already in the repo's luck bars; repeat for any strategy family this source inspires.
11. Claim (paraphrased from a title): A long backtest can reject a system that works over short stretches. Test: nested windows (1, 3, 6 months, full history), as the still-working check does, and count rows that pass short windows but fail the full one.
12. Claim (paraphrased from a title): Robust strategies do not depend on exact settings. Test: for a chosen row, check whether its neighbours (nearby TP, SL and look-back) also make money.
