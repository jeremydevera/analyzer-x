## Claims we could test on our own data

1. Weak breadth before a crash (lesson 1). Build a breadth line from the stored daily candles of every Gate contract — for example, the share of coins above their own 50-day average — and mark days where BTC is near a high while that share is falling. Then compare how often a big BTC drop follows those days against all other days. Note the author's own answer is not on this page; the title only suggests he found the claim weak.

2. Volatility has a memory (lesson 5). For each coin and timeframe, compare each candle's range (high minus low, over the close) with the average range of the next 5 candles. If memory is real, high-range candles are followed by higher-than-normal ranges. This needs only the candles already in the store.

3. From the fetch-tool summary only (not his words, so treat as a lead): after a quiet stretch, volatility does not jump sharply; narrow-range candles are followed by more narrow-range candles; after a shock candle the next candles are calmer than the shock but still above normal. All three can be counted on stored 1h, 4h and 1d candles across all coins and compared with an average candle.

4. Stand aside in choppy markets (lessons 16 and 25). Tag each day per coin as choppy or trending by a rule we choose (the page gives none), then compare the per-trade profit, after fees, of the trades already saved in Backtest v2's trade lists on choppy days against trending days. Choose the choppy rule on older days and check it on newer days, as this repo requires.

5. Summer is slow (lesson 27). Compare average daily range and volume by calendar month for BTC and other long-listed coins, as far back as the daily candles go. Crypto trades every day, so it may not follow the stock-market summer pattern he describes.

6. Relative volume (lesson 22). Test whether a candle whose volume is far above its usual level is followed by a bigger move than normal. The page gives no formula, so this would test our own version of the idea, not his tool.

7. Fibonacci ratios do not help (lesson 14). Count how often price turns at Fibonacci retracement levels of a prior swing compared with randomly placed levels in the same swing. The page names no ratios, so the levels would be our choice.

## What I did not read, and doubts

- I read only the blog's front page. Every post is cut off after its first lines, so I did not read a single full post, its data, its method or its results. Every lesson above is the author's opening claim or a title, not a finding he shows evidence for here.
- Page 2 of the list ("See all") and every linked page were not read.
- The two extra files (5EB4F0CB-tut1.txt and 5EB4F0CB-tut2.txt) are fetch-tool summaries, not the author's words; I used them only for context and quoted nothing from them. Their numbers are unchecked.
- The saved text has broken characters where curly quotes and dashes were (shown as a replacement symbol), so a few sentences could only be quoted in part (lessons 16 and 24).
- Lesson 14 (no belief in Fibonacci) and lesson 31 (psychology matters) are a reader's words quoted by the author, not his own sentences.
- Marketing: "This was a trade that was shared with our MarketLife members in advance" is a pitch for his membership; "Short term trading strategies that work" promises results the excerpt never shows; "Trades like this are easy" sits right beside that pitch. Treat these as selling, not evidence.
- Several posts are about health, AI, writing or a New Year greeting and have nothing to do with trading.
- Everything here is about stocks, stock-index futures and gold; nothing is about crypto, so any claim would need testing before it applies to the coins this repo trades.
