## What it is

This is the "SOFTWARE" page of Marcos Lopez de Prado's website. It is a table that lists the Python files behind many of his papers, so a reader can repeat his results and run their own tests. Each row gives the author, a version date, the file links, the paper the file belongs to, and a one-line description of what the file does. It does not teach any method in detail: it names the methods and points at the papers and code. The topics are backtest overfitting (a simulator, the Probability of Backtest Overfitting, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio), drawdown and time under water, portfolio building (HRP, CLA, ERC, MMSC, EF3M), trading rules for a mean-reverting price, smoothing (KCA) and execution timing.

## What it says on costs, overfitting, testing on unseen data, position size, leverage and stops

**Costs.** Not covered. No file is described as charging fees or slippage. OEH.py is about the best time over which to carry out an order, but the page does not say whether that is about cost.
> "Determination of the optimal execution horizon."

**Overfitting.** This is the page's main theme, but only by name. It offers an online simulator that overfits a seasonal strategy on purpose, two programs that estimate the Probability of Backtest Overfitting, one that simulates overfitting scenarios, and DSR.py, whose paper corrects the Sharpe ratio for picking the best of many tries. None of the descriptions say how the estimate works or what number counts as too much.
> "This online tools overfits a seasonal investment strategy within the parameter ranges specified by the user."

**Testing on unseen data.** Only named. "Out-of-sample" (data not used to build the thing) appears in two paper titles and in HRP_MC.py's description, which says HRP had lower variance than CLA out-of-sample. The page never describes how to hold data back or how to split it.
> "Building Diversified Portfolios that Outperform Out-Of-Sample"

**Position size.** Not covered for single trades. Several files decide how to split money across many assets (HRP, CLA, ERC, MMSC, PSR portfolio optimisation, EF3M), but the page gives no rule for how big one trade should be.
> "HRP.py performs a hierarchical portfolio construction."

**Leverage.** Not covered.

**Stops.** Only named. One paper is about "drawdown-based stop-outs" and a "Triple Penance" rule, and its files compute the biggest drawdown and the longest time under water. The page does not state the rule, and does not say whether a "stop-out" means closing one trade or stopping a whole strategy.
> "Drawdown-based Stop-Outs and the "Triple Penance" rule"

## Claims we could test on our own data

The page does not say what input each file expects, so every idea here means opening the code first.

- **DSR.py (the expected best of many random scores).** This repo tries a great many settings at once: 63 combinations in Backtest v3, 24,480 rule sets in Forecast v4, 242,313 strategies in Backtest v4. DSR.py would tell us how good the BEST of that many tries is expected to look from luck alone. If our best rule set's score is not above that number, its lead is what picking the top of a big list produces. The repo's "random picks" luck bar asks a similar question by simulation; DSR.py would give a formula answer to check it against.
- **CSCV_1.py and CSCV_2.py (Probability of Backtest Overfitting).** Our walk-forward checks found that a rule set's rank barely carries from one stretch of months to the next. Running PBO on the same table of rule sets by day (Forecast v4's per-day results are filed for every rule set) would put one number on how often the best pick on old days lands in the bottom half on new days.
- **CSCV_3.py and the online simulator.** Could be used to see what a seasonal strategy that is pure overfitting looks like, as a picture to compare our own "best" tables with.
- **DD2.py and DD3.py (biggest drawdown and longest time under water when results are linked).** Our rooms already print the worst losing streak and worst dip. These files would give the dip and the time below peak to EXPECT, given a strategy's win rate, average result and how much one result is linked to the next, so we could tell whether a room's dip is normal or a sign it has stopped working.
- **OTR.py (best trading rule for a mean-reverting price, without a backtest).** The leading Forecast v4 rule sets switch on VWAP reversion strategies. If a coin's price really fits the mean-reverting model the file assumes, OTR.py could suggest a stop and target to compare with what our grids chose. We would first have to check that the price fits that model; the page gives no way to check it.
- **PSR.py.** The page links it to the Sharpe ratio but does not say what it measures. It might give a confidence figure on a strategy's Sharpe ratio; that has to be read from the code or paper.
- **HRP, CLA, ERC, MMSC, EF3M, KCA, SFD, OEH.** Less useful here: the rooms stake a flat $5 a trade and do not build a weighted portfolio, and the page says nothing tying KCA, SFD or OEH to backtest checks.
- **Licence doubt.** The code is for non-business use, and for-profit use needs the authors' written approval. Running it to choose strategies that trade real money may count as for-profit use; ask before doing that.

- The saved text shows two broken characters around the words "as is" in the licence note (the quote marks were lost in saving), so I did not quote that phrase.

- The page has typos that I kept in the quotes: "This online tools overfits", "Enthough has built", "pre-authorized in written", "close-formula", and a missing verb in "DD3.py the maximum of the Time under Water".
