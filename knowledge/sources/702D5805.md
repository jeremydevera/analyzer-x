- **Type:** html (a documentation page)
- **Free or paid:** the page says every account is free, with paid "Power" and "Commercial" plans for higher limits or business use; it shows no prices
- **Words read:** 1,213 of 1,213 (this count includes the menu's link text)
- Rendered in a browser on Nov 19, 2026

## What it is

The opening page of Tiingo's data documentation: how the service is split up, how you sign in, what limits apply,
what file formats come back, how stock names are written, and what you may do with the data. Its menu lists the data
it sells: US stock prices of several kinds, news, company fundamentals, fund fees, dividends, splits, and also
**crypto and forex** - so the old note's "only US stock data" was wrong. Every data type has its own page, and none of
those pages were read; this one only names them.

## Everything it teaches

1. The service says it is built to be fast, consistent and filterable.
   > "Tiingo's APIs are built to be performant, consistent, and also support extensive filters to speed up your development time."

2. There are two ways to get data: ask for it (REST, mainly for history, end-of-day data) or hold a live stream open
   (websocket, for real-time data).
   > "REST Endpoints which provide a RESTful interface to querying data, especially historical data for end-of-day data feeds."
   > "Websocket which provide a websocket interface and are used to stream real-time data."

3. If you want every update as it happens, the stream is the way.
   > "If you are looking for access to a raw firehose of data, the websocket endpoint is how you get it."

4. The "ask for it" side covers eleven data types: End-of-Day, News, Crypto, Forex, 24x5 Consolidated, IEX, BOATS
   Overnight, Fundamentals, Fund Fees, Dividends and Splits.
   > "2.1 End-of-Day 2.2 News - /url: /documentation/news 2.3 Crypto - /url: /documentation/crypto 2.4 Forex"
   > "2.8 Fundamentals - /url: /documentation/fundamentals 2.9 Fund Fees"

5. The live stream covers five: Crypto, Forex, 24x5 Consolidated, IEX and BOATS Overnight.
   > "3.1 Crypto - /url: /documentation/websockets/crypto 3.2 Forex"

6. There is also a search tool and an appendix on a developer program, integrations and how names are written.
   > "4.1 Search - /url: /documentation/utilities/search"

7. Other people have written ready-made packages that read Tiingo data (the list itself did not come through in the
   saved text).
   > "there are a number of third-party packages that interface with our data and make it even easier to get up and running with our data"

8. You must make an account; every account is free, and you pay only for higher limits or business use.
   > "In order to use the API, you must sign-up to create an account. All accounts are free and if you need higher usage limits, or you have a commercial use case, you can upgade to the Power and/or Commercial plan."

9. Your account gets one secret key (a "token") that stands in for your username and password, so guard it.
   > "This token is used in place of your username & password throughout the API, so keep it safe like you would your password."

10. Limits are counted three ways: requests per hour, requests per day, and data downloaded per month.
    > "Hourly Requests - Reset every hour. Daily Requests - Reset every day at midnight EST. Monthly Bandwidth - Reset the first of every month at midnight EST."

11. There is no per-minute or per-second limit.
    > "We do not rate limit to minute or second, so you are free to make your requests as you desire."

12. Each plan has different limits, but the actual numbers are on the pricing page, not here.
    > "The basic, power, and commercial power plans offer different levels of rate limits. To see what these rate limits are, visit the pricing page"

13. You can email sales for custom limits, and long-time users may get more for free.
    > "Chances are, if you've been with us for some time and the request is reasonable, we will increase your limits at no charge."

14. Most "ask for it" data can come back as JSON (roomier, carries extra info, but bigger and slower) or CSV (plain
    columns, often 4-5 times faster).
    > "This format allows the most flexibility as we can append meta data as well as debugging data. The downside to this data type is that it requires more bandwidth"
    > "data return type that is often 4-5x faster than JSON"

15. You pick the format with a setting called `format`, and each data page says which formats it supports.
    > "To return data in a particular format, you may pass the format parameter"
    > "When browsing the documentation, you will see at the top of the page which return formats are supported."

16. Stock names use a dash, not a dot, for share classes (Berkshire class A is BRK-A).
    > "Tiingo's symbol format uses dashes"
    > "For example Berkshire class A shares would be"

17. A full list of the names it covers is a zip file, refreshed daily.
    > "a full list of tickers can be found in supported_tickers.zip"
    > "which is updated daily"

18. On the free (Basic) and Power plans the data is for your own private use and may not be passed on in any form.
    > "For Basic and Power accounts, data is for internal and personal use only. You may not redistribute the data in any form."

19. On the Commercial plan it may be used inside a business, but still not passed on.
    > "For Commercial accounts, data is licensed for internal commercial usage."

20. Showing the data to others - a presentation, a website, an app - needs a separate license, asked for by email
    with your use case, a link to your firm, and your company size.
    > "If you would like to redistribute the data for commercial or personal use, for example: a presentation, a proposal, a website or app, or any other usage case, please E-mail"
    > "Whether your company is a start-up (less than 5 employees) or enterprise (5 or more employees)."

21. That license is a flat price and comes with much higher limits.
    > "All of our redistributable pricing is based on a flat rate, so it is predictable, simple, and easy."
    > "redistributable licenses come with substantially higher usage limits"

22. A program where each user brings their own Tiingo key, and which does not hand out the data, needs no license talk.
    > "requires users to submit their own Tiingo API token in order to use your software, and your software is not distributing our data, you do not need to contact us regarding licensing."

## Numbers and rules it gives

- Hourly limit resets every hour:

- Monthly download allowance resets on the 1st at midnight US Eastern time:
  > "Monthly Bandwidth - Reset the first of every month at midnight EST."

  > "We do not rate limit to minute or second"

  > "often 4-5x faster than JSON"

  > "We support two different return formats:"

  > "(less than 5 employees) or enterprise (5 or more employees)"

  > "data is for internal and personal use only"

  > "To see what these rate limits are, visit the pricing page"

## Relevance to this app

Small. This app trades crypto perpetual futures on Gate and already reads Gate's own candles, order books and funding,
which is what every backtest and practice trade is priced from. The page does list Crypto (both ask-for-it and live
stream) and Forex, so Tiingo could at most be a second price source to cross-check a coin's history - but the page
does not say whether its crypto data covers futures, funding, order books, which exchanges, how far back it goes, or
what candle sizes it has, so nothing here shows it could replace or improve any Gate reading. Its US stock data
(End-of-Day, IEX, 24x5 Consolidated, BOATS Overnight) is the one possibly useful angle: Gate trades stock tokens, and a
stock's home-market price and hours could help judge them - but the page only names those feeds. One rule matters if
it is ever used: on the free and Power plans the data may not be passed on, and the page counts "a website or app" as
passing it on, so Tiingo numbers should never go onto a shared artifact page without a license.
