## What it is

The authors' own home page for the second edition (February 2009) of a statistics textbook by three Stanford professors,
Trevor Hastie, Robert Tibshirani and Jerome Friedman. The page is a frame: a menu on the left (about, order, contents,
data, errata, R functions, complements, teachers' info, reviews, links) and a main frame that names the book and offers
the free PDF, corrected in its 12th printing of Jan 2017. The about page says the book explains learning from data, from
predicting an outcome to finding structure with no outcome, in one shared framework, with ideas ahead of maths. It is not a
trading book: finance is named once, as one of the fields where data piled up. For this app the useful part is the table
of contents, which shows a whole chapter on judging a model on data it never saw (chapter 7) and a section on testing
many things at once (18.7).

## Everything it teaches

What follows comes from the about page and the chapter and section TITLES only. A one-line description of a chapter is
my reading of its titles, not of its text. Chapters marked **[TESTING]** bear on how strategies are tested: model
assessment and selection, cross-validation, bootstrap, over-fitting, regularization (holding a model back), multiple
testing and the false discovery rate.

From the about page:

1. The book puts the ideas of statistics, data mining and machine learning into one shared frame, with the stress on ideas, not maths.
   > "This book descibes the important ideas in these areas in a common conceptual framework."
2. It covers both predicting an outcome ("supervised") and finding patterns with no outcome ("unsupervised").
   > "The book's coverage is broad, from supervised learning (prediction) to unsupervised learning."
3. The second edition added many topics the first did not have.
   > "This major new edition features many topics not covered in the original, including graphical models, random forests, ensemble methods, least angle regression & path algorithms for the lasso, non-negative matrix factorization and spectral clustering."
4. One chapter is about data with far more inputs than cases, and it includes testing many things at once. **[TESTING]**
   > "including multiple testing and false discovery rates"

## What it says on costs, overfitting, testing on unseen data, position size, leverage and stops

All of this is from chapter and section titles only; no text of the book was read.

**Costs.** No title mentions trading costs, fees or the gap between buy and sell prices. The nearest titles are
"Statistical Decision Theory" (2.4) and "Loss Functions and Robustness" (10.6), which by their words are about how a
mistake is scored, not about money paid to an exchange.

**Overfitting.** Several titles name it: 11.5.2 (neural networks), 15.3.4 (random forests and over-fitting) and 16.2.3
(regularization paths, over-fitting and margins), though the PDF text breaks those three words, so they are not quoted.
Chapter 7's "Optimism of the Training Error Rate" (7.4) says by its title alone that a model looks better on the data it
was fitted on than it will on new data. The regularization sections (3.4, 5, 10.12, 16.2, 18.3, 18.4) are, by their
titles, the book's ways to hold a model back.

**Testing on unseen data.** This is the heart of chapter 7: cross-validation (7.10), with a section on doing it the wrong
and the right way (7.10.2) and one asking whether it really works (7.10.3); bootstrap methods (7.11); and whether the
test error you measure is the one you care about (7.12, "Conditional or Expected Test Error?"). Random forests add "out of
bag samples" (15.3.1). No title says how any of these handle data that comes in time order, like price candles.

**Position size.** Nothing. No title is about sizing a bet.

**Leverage.** Nothing.

**Stops.** Nothing. (Chapter 9 has "Missing Data" and chapter 18 has "Asymmetric Cutpoints", but neither title is about
stop-loss prices.)

## Which chapters to read for this app

This app measures millions of settings (coin x timeframe x signal x target x stop), chooses some on older days and judges
them on unseen days. Five places in this book have titles that name that exact problem:

1. **18.7, "Feature Assessment and the Multiple-Testing Problem", with 18.7.1 "The False Discovery Rate" and 18.7.3
   "A Bayesian Interpretation of the FDR"** (pages 683 to 692). When you test 12,527,659 Backtest v2 rows, or 28,717,380
   confluence candidates, some will look great by luck alone. The app has already met this: in the Pro Trader research 3
   of 1,900 random picks passed the money checks against 3 of 2,255 real ones, and the v3 proof uses one strict cut for
   63 combinations. A "false discovery rate", by its words, is the share of finds that are false, which is the number
   the app's luck bars try to estimate. This is the first section to read.
2. **Chapter 7, especially 7.4 "Optimism of the Training Error Rate", 7.10.2 "The Wrong and Right Way to Do
   Cross-validation", 7.10.3 and 7.12** (pages 228 to 257). The app found on Nov 05, 2026 that all 8 ways of choosing on
   the older 15 days lost money on the newest 15; 7.4's title names why a chosen setting looks better than it trades.
   7.10.2 promises a common mistake, worth reading before trusting any choose-then-check pipeline (one draft of the
   all-coins page ranked its "BEST" by the very days it was judged on).
3. **7.11 "Bootstrap Methods" and chapter 8's bootstrap sections (8.2, 8.4).** The v3 proof already uses a
   day-clustered bootstrap and the skill checks a 200-draw Monte Carlo; these sections are where the method is explained.
   Whether they cover data in time order (days that depend on each other) the titles do not say.
4. **8.7 Bagging, 8.8 "Model Averaging and Stacking", 8.9 "Stochastic Search: Bumping", chapter 16 "Ensemble Learning"
   and "Bet on Sparsity" (16.2.2).** The daily picker hands each room ONE rule set; these titles are about combining many
   models instead of trusting one winner, the other path the app has not tried.
5. **3.3 subset selection, 3.4 shrinkage and 3.6 "Discussion: A Comparison of the Selection and Shrinkage Methods", and
   18.1 "When p is Much Bigger than N".** The confluence search picks a few switches out of many (5 triggers x 7
   confirmations), and a coin-timeframe with 30 days of candles carries thousands of measured settings: more knobs than
   independent cases. Those titles are about exactly that.

Also worth a look by title: 2.9 and 7.2 (model complexity: every extra knob is a chance to fit noise) and 9.3 "PRIM: Bump
Hunting", which by its name searches for regions where the outcome is high, as the room rules search for corners of win
rate and trade count. That last one is a guess from three words.

## What I did not read, and doubts

- **I read only the frame menu, the about page, the contents list, the teachers' page, the reviews list and the
  complements list.** Not one page of the book's own text. Every chapter description above is a reading of titles.
- **Not saved:** the order page, data page, errata, R functions page, "what's new in the 2nd edition" page, links page,
  the authors' other book link (sldm), and the download page (documented as 7CD6075B). The reviews page lists reviewers
  (David Hand, Eric Ziegel, Jim Ramsay and others) but none of the reviews themselves.
- **The PDF text is damaged in places:** "fi" comes out as "ﬂ", "ff" as "ﬁ", a dash as "{" and the "much bigger than" sign
  in chapter 18's title as "pÀN". So the titles of chapter 4, chapter 18, 2.9, 5.5.2, 7.3, 7.6, 10.7, 11.5.2, 15.3.4 and 16.2.3 could not be
  quoted whole; I quoted only clean runs.
- **Page numbers:** the contents PDF opens with "This is page xiii", and I cannot tell which printing its page numbers
  belong to. The about page says 763 pages; the index starts on page 737.
- **Fit to trading:** no chapter title mentions time series, prices, or data in time order. The about page names finance
  once, as a field with lots of data. Whether the book's cross-validation and bootstrap advice holds for candles, where
  today depends on yesterday, cannot be told from the titles; the book's text has to be read for that.
- **Age:** the book is from 2009 with corrections to 2017; the newest thing on these pages is a review "discovered in 2023".
- The complements page summarises the authors' later papers (coordinate descent for the lasso, a NIPS challenge study,
  a penalized matrix decomposition, shrinkage in the Cox model). They are not chapters of the book and were not read.
