- **Free or paid:** the page does not say what Zorro costs; this contents page is free to read online. It points to a book (the "Black Book") without saying its price.
- **Words read:** 318 of 318 (all of the saved text, plus every link in the saved raw page).

## What it is

This is the table of contents of the manual for Zorro, a program for writing and testing trading strategies.
It lists the program's modes (testing, training, trading and more), its feature topics, a C programming tutorial
made of numbered "workshops", a set of ready-made strategies called the Z Systems, and a list of functions.
It explains nothing itself: every topic is a link to another page, and none of those pages was saved.
It ends by telling the reader not to worry about the number of functions, because the defaults work.

## Numbers and rules it gives

No trading numbers. The only numbers are the workshop numbers (1 to 8, plus 4a). Usage rules:

- "If in doubt, use the local manual."
- Open the local manual with the Help button or "[ Shift-F1 ] in the editor".
- Search with the "[ Search ]" tab; find a function or variable with the "[ Index ]" tab.
- "you'll only need a small subset of the available functions"

## What it says on costs, overfitting, testing on unseen data, position size, leverage and stops

**Costs:** not covered. "HFT Simulation" and "Broker Arbitrage" are titles only; nothing about fees, gaps or slippage.

**Overfitting:** not covered. "Oversampling", "Montecarlo Analysis" and "Retraining" are listed as titles, with no word on what they guard against.

**Testing on unseen data:** not covered. "Testing" and "Training" are named as two modes, but the page does not say which data each uses.

**Position size:** not covered. "Money Management" (file `optimalf.htm`), "Mean-Variance Optimization" and "Workshop 6: Portfolio Managing" (file `tutorial_kelly.htm`) are titles only.

**Leverage:** not covered.

**Stops:** not covered.
