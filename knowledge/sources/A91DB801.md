## What it is

This is the publisher's page for a 79-page book by Lawrence E. Harris, PhD, CFA, dated 1 October 2015. The page gives a short description of what the book covers and links to the full book and a summary, both as PDFs. Its main idea is that you should trade only when you have a real reason to expect a gain, and because trading is zero-sum (one side's gain is the other side's loss), you must also understand why the person on the other side trades. Most of the page names topics the book covers (where trading costs come from, when active strategies make money, why speed matters in electronic trading) without giving the book's answers.

## Claims we could test on our own data

1. Zero-sum: if trading is zero-sum before costs, random entries on our coins should average about $0.00 a trade before fees and lose about the cost of a round trip after them. We can check this on the stored backtest trades, before fees and after.
2. Costs decide whether you do well: re-price the same trades at the coin's usual gap and at each minute's own recorded order book, then compare the profit per trade.
3. Speed matters: for the same signals, compare the price we get entering 3 seconds after a candle closes against entering later (for example a minute later).
