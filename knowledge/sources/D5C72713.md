## What it is

The first of ten lecture decks for López de Prado's Cornell course on financial machine learning (documented as
BCA2B653). CXO Advisory summarised lectures 1 to 9 in one page; lecture 1 is the part headed "Financial Machine
Learning". Read with B60874BA (his full book, read), BCA2B653 and D1E57A8C, the series says the same thing: most
backtests are false because people try many things and keep the best.

## Everything it teaches (as summarised by CXO Advisory, not the slides)

**Lecture 1, "Financial Machine Learning"**

1. Machine learning is statistics with computers, for problems with many variables.
   > "Machine learning is statistics for the age of computing, tackling complex patterns driven by many variables without specific direction."
2. It can help choose how much to hold, and spot changes.
   > "Machine learning is effective for signal generation, detecting pattern change, bet sizing, outlier detection, replicating human analyst recommendations"
3. **It invites overfitting, so use it to find what matters, not to tune a backtest.**
   > "However, machine learning invites backtest overfitting. It should be used to discover what factors are important in support of theory development."

**The rest of the series (lectures 2 to 9)**

4. Measure time by trading activity, not the clock.
   > "Measure in intervals of information arrival (such as trading volume or dollars traded) rather than calendar time."
5. A backtest must include stops, profit-taking and margin calls.
   > "Account for profit-taking, stop-outs and margin calls in backtests."
6. Decide direction and size separately.
   > "Model position direction and position size separately."
7. **Keep every training day out of the test.**
   > "Rigorously exclude all in-sample (training) data from the out-of-sample test dataset, with focus on variables formed from overlapping observations."
8. Test each input on its own.
   > "Test each feature of a model independently to assess its importance."
9. Do not trade on every tiny signal change.
   > "Impose discrete steps on trade triggers to avoid overtrading."
10. **Build for a whole asset class, not one market.**
    > "Develop models for entire asset classes, not individual assets."
11. **Count your backtests, and test many scenarios, not one walk-forward.**
    > "Keep track of backtests used on each dataset. Test many simulated scenarios ( cross-validation ), not just one history (a single walk-forward)."
12. **Do not tweak a strategy to improve its backtest.**
    > "Resist the impulse to tweak a strategy to improve its backtest."
13. Judge a strategy by more than profit, and correct the Sharpe ratio for snooping.
    > "versions of Sharpe ratio corrected for non- normal returns, short samples and data snooping bias"
14. Compare what was predicted with what happened.
    > "Track also predicted versus actual outcomes."
15. Estimate the chance the strategy will fail.
    > "Estimate the probability that the overall strategy will fail over time."
16. **"The 7 Reasons Most Machine Learning Funds Fail"** include ignoring stops, overlapping training and test data, and
    reusing data without correcting for it.
    > "Ignoring stop-outs/margin calls and profit-taking rules in backtests."
    > "Allowing overlap of training (in-sample) and testing (out-of-sample) datasets."
    > "Reusing data across multiple tests without accounting for associated data snooping bias."

## What it means for this app

Points 10, 11 and 16 are this app's open problem: strategies are chosen coin by coin from millions of settings, with
one walk-forward history, and nothing counts the tries. Point 5 the app already does (stops and liquidation are in its
backtests). Point 9 matches the cost check and 2:1 rule, which refuse trades too small for their costs.
