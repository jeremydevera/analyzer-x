It lists 85 article entries under 17 headings, from beginner guides to backtesting, exits, stops, risk, live trading problems, scams and psychology. The opening paragraph says the articles are aimed at traders who use TradeStation's EasyLanguage. Everything below is therefore what each TITLE or TEASER says the article is about, never what the article actually concludes.

## Claims we could test on our own data

The page makes few claims, so most items below are questions it raises that our stores could answer; the article's own answer is unknown to us.

1. "90% or more winning trades" (entry 27). Take every Backtest v2 row that won 90%+ on its older days and measure what it made after every fee on its newest days, against rows at lower win rates. Our rooms already switch on at 80-90%, so this tests our own rule as much as his question.
2. "Pick Up Pennies, Get Steamrolled" (entry 60): small, frequent wins hide big losses. For high-win-rate rows with a target smaller than the stop, compare the worst losing run in dollars with the total profit, and count how often one run wipes out the whole gain.
3. Dollar stops against ATR stops (entry 38). Our grid uses fixed percent stops; add a stop set at k times the coin's average true range and compare on unseen days, coin by coin, after fees.
4. Breakeven stops (entry 40). Add a rule that moves the stop to the entry price after a gain of x%, replay with minute exits, and compare profit and win rate on unseen days.
5. Best day of the week (entry 9). Group existing trades by UTC weekday of entry and check whether any day's profit per trade differs from the others by more than a shuffled-day bootstrap would give.
6. Trade only with the trend (entry 44). Split trades by whether they went with a long-term trend (for example BTC above its 200-day average, or the coin's own) and compare unseen-day profit. Backtest v3 already has a BTC mood filter to reuse.
7. Quick exits with a profit (entry 56). Our grid already measures small targets; compare small-target rows against wider ones on unseen days, with the cost-to-target ratio beside each, since fees eat a small target first.
8. Equity curve trading (entry 48) and "When To Quit Trading A Strategy" (entry 22). Replay a rule that switches a strategy off when its own running total falls below its average (or out of a Monte Carlo cone, entry 46) and check whether the trades it skipped really lost.
9. Diversification as the "Holy Grail" (entry 47). Build portfolios of strategies on weakly linked coins and compare the worst drop against single strategies of the same total profit.
10. Risk of ruin and the 2% rule (entries 50, 61). From a strategy's own win rate and win/loss sizes, compute the chance of losing a $65-$100 account at $5 a trade and at 2% a trade, and check it against shuffled replays.
11. "Simple >> Complex" (entry 52). Compare rules with one condition against rules with several (the confluence research's combinations) on unseen days.
12. Closing-price fills work only in backtests (entry 19). Our analogue: fills at the candle's last price against fills at the recorded order book of the same minute. The repo already found this gap on the skill rooms; a wider check would repeat it on Backtest v2 rows.
13. Scale trading drains the account (entry 81). Replay adding to a losing position against flat size; the repo already measures a doubling ladder against flat.
14. Data changes hurt live algos (entries 63, 65). Re-download a month of candles and compare closed candles value by value with the saved copy; count how many signals would have changed.
15. Not testable from this page: the 15 price patterns (entry 42), the 2 metrics (29), the one metric (58), peel off trading (83) and the 3 mistakes (10), because the page never names them.
